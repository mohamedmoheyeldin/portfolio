Use this guide to explain the projects in this repository during an interview. The complete question bank, story variants, reviewer worksheets and practice log remain in the sibling career project so there is one maintained version.

## Start here

1. Read the [main interview guide](../../fde-career-transition/interview/fde-practice/README.md).
2. Choose a project from the mapping below.
3. Rehearse its short story, explain your personal contribution, then show only shareable portfolio evidence.
4. Use the [technical walkthrough](INTERVIEW-TECHNICAL-WALKTHROUGH.md) for questions about the portfolio itself.
5. Record the session in the [career practice log](../../fde-career-transition/interview/README.md#priority-and-practice-record).

Sibling links require the career repository beside this repository under C:\Projects. They are local workspace links, not public website URLs. This guide is repository documentation, not a website route or download. Keep private reviewer comments and raw employer evidence in the career project's ignored private area.

## Five-minute presentation

**0:00–0:30 — Positioning.** Explain how you connect user workflows, implementation, integration, validation and adoption. Use factual employment titles.

**0:30–2:00 — Referral tool.** State the recurring preparation problem, your generation/edit/upload work, lower-environment scope, demonstrations and daily use. Keep the reported result at its supported precision.

**2:00–3:00 — Separate VA application contribution.** Explain stakeholder requirements, React features, MySQL investigation and Playwright checks. Distinguish shared platform work from independently developed tooling.

**3:00–4:00 — Shareable implementation.** Show the portfolio's shared career record, validator and rendering pipeline. Explain the tradeoff between shared facts and format-specific presentation.

**4:00–5:00 — Invite a focused follow-up.** “Would you like to go deeper into implementation, the customer workflow, or how people adopted the tool?”

Explain employer work through public-safe narratives. Do not improvise private endpoints, customer data or a working demo that does not exist.

## Answers to likely portfolio questions

### Which is your best FDE example?

“The referral tool is my strongest example of building around a recurring user need. I independently developed and maintain it, demonstrated changes, documented workflows and supported daily QA use. My VA application work adds direct stakeholder requirements and frontend contributions. Those are related but distinct scopes.”

### What did you build versus the team?

“I independently built and maintain Data Generator & File Processing. I contributed features and automation to the shared VA platform. The portfolio is my independent project. I would explain each responsibility separately rather than claim the entire customer platform.”

### Where is the proof of the outcome?

“Daily QA use and hundreds of referrals in minutes are reported outcomes. The portfolio explains scope and contribution; it is not an independent benchmark. For the portfolio itself, I can explain the implementation and local checks. I do not have a measured hiring outcome.”

### Why does quality engineering prepare you for FDE work?

“It taught me to make requirements concrete, investigate systems across boundaries and explain risk with evidence. My application and tooling work adds implementation and adoption. I would demonstrate those actions rather than rely on a title to establish the fit.”
