# Design brief: the casebook

The site is a deck of case files. The first screen says who he is and what he does, and shows the tops of three case sheets along its bottom edge. Scrolling lays those sheets one over another. Each sheet you pass leaves its folder tab showing, so by the third sheet the row of tabs is an index of what you have read, and any tab takes you back. The identity is a cool, quiet ground with white sheets, one signal colour (teal) and IBM Plex. It replaced the diff build (workflow diffs and a command palette) on October 30, 2026; the full Scrollcraft brief is in [SCROLLCRAFT-BRIEF.md](SCROLLCRAFT-BRIEF.md).

- **Cover.** Open-to-roles status, then the `h1`: the name on one line and the claim at display scale ("I work with the people who use the software, build what they need, and test it before they do."). Below, the role and experience in one sentence, Email me, the resume popover and See the work, beside a fact sheet (now, experience, domains, LinkedIn and GitHub). The cover fills the first screen; its last row is the three case tabs (number, short name, result value), which jump to each case.
- **Signature move: the case deck.** Three featured projects as sheets, each with a folder tab at its own third of the row. On screens at least 900px wide and 700px tall, the sheets stick under the header and the next one slides over the last. Every sheet keeps the same height, so only the earlier tabs show above the current sheet. A tab jumps back to its sheet through an anchor in normal flow (a sticky element cannot be scrolled to reliably). `enhance.ts` turns stacking off when any sheet's content would be clipped at the current size and fonts. Phones, short screens and visitors without JavaScript get the same sheets one after another. Pointer events pass through the transparent tab row so earlier tabs stay clickable.
- **A case sheet.** On the left: period and role, the project name, the one-line summary (dropped on screens under 940px tall while stacked, since the case study carries it), the result with its evidence tag, the problem, and Read the case study plus the source link. On the right: the documented design as a pipeline (numbered stations on one rail, then what runs alongside), and the tools.
- **Navigation.** A floating island: the name and role, Work, Experience, Contact (the section in view is marked on the home page), the theme switch and Email me. Below 860px the routes move into a native popover menu that also lists every resume file.
- **Earlier and supporting work.** The other three projects as rows: number and period, name and summary, result and evidence.
- **Career.** A tinted band with a timeline: dates in mono, a rail that draws as it passes, and the current role marked in teal. **What I bring:** six capabilities, each restating documented highlights (discovery, integration, application development, workflow automation, test automation, demos and enablement), then the tools by group and the credential line.
- **Close.** The last sheet on every page, with a "Next step" tab: the question, the address set large as the action with a copy button, Email me and the resume popover; LinkedIn, GitHub and all four resume files beside it; the two-week AI workflow audit offer across the bottom. It is the same white sheet as the cases, not an inverted plate.
- **Type.** IBM Plex Sans (variable weight; text and display) and IBM Plex Mono (labels, numbers, dates). Two families, self-hosted Latin subsets with metric-matched fallbacks, 60 KB in total.
- **Colour.** Light: ground `#eef0f3`, sheets `#ffffff`, ink `#0d1117`, teal `#0a6b5d`. Dark: ground `#0b0d10`, sheets `#15191f`, ink `#eef1f5`, teal `#46d4b8`. Green and red appear only in self-test results. Every ink token clears 4.5:1 on every ground in both themes (lowest 5.15:1 light, 6.01:1 dark; the self-test measures it).
- **Depth and surfaces.** The ground is quiet; sheets carry the content with a hairline border, and stacked sheets cast a shadow upward onto the one beneath. Panels are used only for objects: sheets, the pipeline, the fact sheet, credential cards, the self-test.
- **Motion.** Native CSS only, inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; scroll-driven parts also inside `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`. The cover arrives in reading order and its tabs rise from the edge; the deck is plain sticky positioning (it works with motion off); section heads, rows, capabilities and the closing sheet rise as they enter; the pipeline and career rails draw; the island gains depth after the first scroll. The status dot pulses three times and stops. Transform, opacity and shadow only; no scroll-jacking, no smooth scrolling.
- **Content rules.** Career facts come from `career.json`; no invented numbers, logos, generated imagery or mock interfaces. Diagrams restate documented fields and say so. No em dashes in visible copy.
